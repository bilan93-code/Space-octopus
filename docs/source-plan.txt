 The controller uses STM32G431RBT6 + DRV8353SRTAR + six CSD19536KTT MOSFETs, with three
 low-side phase-current shunts. It includes the supply rails, bus monitoring, hardware shutdown, CAN,
 debugging, temperature inputs, and a regenerative brake chopper. One purchased FLUX IND-ROT-045
 motor-shaft encoder connects through a differential BiSS-C interface. The encoder itself and torque sensor
 are external assemblies.

 Read this document together with PCB_BOM_RevA.csv, the three pin-assignment CSV files, Net_Classes.csv
 and tscircuit_build_brief.md. The JSON file preserves the numerical design basis. BOM rows with provisional
 status deliberately identify parts that cannot be ordered responsibly until dimensions, cable requirements or
 fault energy are known.

 The report's 22 Arms continuous holding calculation reaches about 154.7 C and fails its own limit. Its
 five-second 22 Arms pulse case starts from specific lower-current thermal states. Repeated pulses, different
 mounting and an already hot motor require a new thermal duty assessment. This PCB must not label 22
 Arms as a continuous motor rating.

 For balanced sinusoidal currents, I_peak = sqrt(2) × I_rms. Thus 13 Arms corresponds to 18.38 A peak and
 22 Arms to 31.11 A peak. An amplitude-invariant Park transform uses peak-equivalent dq current; the study's
 RMS convention must be converted explicitly. DC supply current is a different quantity; the study's
 approximately 394 W electrical case corresponds to about 8.2 A at 48 V before inverter losses.

 This is one common-ground motor drive. The gate driver, inverter and MCU belong together on the controller
 PCB even when the motor is mechanically separate from the gearbox. Keep the board near the motor to
 shorten phase wiring. The motor housing and cable shield require an enclosure bonding plan.

 The upstream power source must provide controlled precharge or a slow voltage ramp, correct polarity, a
 suitable disconnect and current limiting. These are explicit system boundaries of this first board. A keyed
 connector is not electronic reverse-polarity protection. Do not insert a one-way series protection diode
 without reassessing regeneration and the brake circuit.

 Start with a 120 × 100 mm planning envelope, four copper layers, and space for four radial bus
 capacitors. These are layout allowances, not a solved mechanical fit. Place the board on a cooled bracket or
 enclosure surface; the motor study's thermal boundary does not establish PCB cooling.


 3. Selected FLUX motor encoder
 Use IND-ROT-045-C2115-19-BIS10-AL as the proposed configuration to confirm with FLUX. It is an
 inductive absolute encoder, with a 45 mm stator and a 15 mm rotor bore in this mounting variant. A
 precision 12-to-15 mm split adapter is needed for the study's 12 mm shaft. Check concentricity, the existing
 19 mm locknut envelope, and rear-cap axial clearance in CAD before freezing the mount. [E1]

 This is my preferred fit for motor commutation because it accommodates the shaft through an adapter and
 offers enough resolution without moving to the larger GMI families. Its 19-bit output gives a theoretical step
 of 0.000687 mechanical degrees, or 0.00343 electrical degrees with five pole pairs. Resolution is not
 accuracy. Use the published standard ±0.040° accuracy as the baseline; the tighter ±0.025° figure depends
 on improved mounting. [E1,E2]

 Reserve a protected rear-end mounting region with the nominal 0.50 mm sensing gap. Keep the encoder
 below its +85 C operating limit; a target below 75 C provides initial margin. It cannot be placed on the 130 C
 winding hotspot. The sensor may need an end-cap spacer and a thermal path separate from the windings.
 [E1]

 Power the encoder from fused 12 V. Its published supply range is 4.35–36 V, so neither the 48 V bus nor the
 3.3 V rail is suitable. Allocate 200 mA at 12 V as a provisional rail budget; the public current figures are at
 other supply voltages and exclude termination loads. Obtain the exact 12 V maximum and startup current
 before finalizing F3. [E1]

 The encoder's own calibration does not determine the motor's electrical zero. During commissioning,
 determine phase order, direction and electrical offset. Use theta_e = 5 × theta_motor + theta_offset,
 wrapped to one electrical revolution. A single-turn motor encoder also does not establish absolute gearbox
 output position after arbitrary unpowered motion. Joint-level absolute position would require an output
 encoder or an appropriate homing strategy.


 4. DC input, bus capacitors and power budgeting
 J1 brings DC_IN and GND onto the PCB. F1 feeds VBUS. D1, the capacitor bank, bleeder, inverter and
 regulator input connect downstream of F1. Place the TVS beside the input loop, and the ceramic
 commutation capacitors directly beside their half bridges. All motor return current goes back to the bus
 capacitors through short, broad copper paths.

 Four starting 470 uF capacitors give 1880 uF. Stored energy at 48 V is 2.17 J. Only about 0.95 J is added
 between 48 V and the proposed 57.6 V braking threshold; capacitance therefore cannot replace the braking
 resistor. Specify the capacitor bank by both capacitance and ripple capability, with a provisional combined
 target of at least 12 Arms at the actual switching frequency and case temperature. Confirm this from
 simulated/measured bus-current waveforms. Increase the count or change capacitor technology if selected
 parts cannot meet the target.

 The 22k bleeder dissipates about 0.105 W at 48 V. With 1880 uF, its time constant is 41.4 s, so the bus
 remains charged after disconnection. The fuse does not replace semiconductor overcurrent detection. F1's
 20 A value is a starting selection; its DC interrupt rating must exceed the actual source's available fault
 current.

 Use 100 V MOSFETs, bus capacitors and a 100 V input buck. The SMCJ54A is a transient absorber; its
 published pulse clamp is around 87 V at its specified test current. Real wiring inductance and pulse energy
 still determine the voltage at the MOSFET and IC pins. Target measured power-node overshoot below 90 V,
 with documented margin to every relevant absolute maximum. A 100 V label alone does not prove surge
 survival. [D2,D3,D4]

 5. Regulator schematic details
 U3, LM5164, generates V12. Follow the manufacturer's 12 V application topology, using the listed 68 uH
 inductor, 100k RON, 448k/49.9k output divider and 2.2 nF bootstrap capacitor. Keep the three-component
 ripple network: SW → 453k → RIPPLE12; RIPPLE12 → 3.3 nF → V12; RIPPLE12 → 56 pF → FB12. The calculated
 nominal output is 11.974 V. Tie EN/UVLO to VBUS for this baseline. PGOOD contributes to FAULT_RAW_N.
 [D4]

 U4, TPS62160, generates V5. Its 2.2 uH inductor connects SW to V5; VOS senses V5 at the output
 capacitor. The feedback divider is 523k over 100k, for 4.984 V nominal. Connect AGND and PGND to the
 same ground system using the layout arrangement in its datasheet. PG contributes to FAULT_RAW_N. [D5]

 U5, TLV75533, generates V3V3. Tie EN to V5 and decouple both sides. U19, REF3025E, generates
 REF25 from V5 for the independent voltage thresholds. The ADC reference remains VDDA3V3, not REF25.
 The MCU measures REF25 to check ADC scaling. [D6,D7]

 6. MCU, clock, programming and default state
 Use the STM32G431RBT6 in LQFP64. It provides complementary timer outputs, two ADCs and FDCAN
 suitable for this control architecture. The included 64-row table assigns every physical pin. Of particular
 importance, PB15 uses AF4 for TIM1_CH3N; PB13/PB14 use AF6. [D1]

 Decouple every VDD individually with 100 nF. Add local bulk capacitance, filter VDDA, and decouple VREF+.
 Tie VBAT to V3V3. VSSA is a quiet connection to the same ground plane. The 8 MHz oscillator drives PF0 in
 HSE bypass mode through 33 ohm; PF1 is unused. Connect SWD and a 3.3 V UART header for bring-up. Set
 boot option bytes explicitly and keep BOOT0 low by default.

 At reset, external pulldowns hold DRV_ENABLE, MCU_RUN and ARM_PULSE low. U10's output enable defaults
 high, disabling all six PWM buffers. The six U2 input pulldowns then command zero gate activity. CAN
 defaults to standby. The driver must be awake for setup and current-offset calibration while PWM remains
 blocked.

 There is no need for a second application computer on this PCB. A Jetson or other robot computer sends
 motion commands over CAN; the STM32 closes the fast current loop locally. The motor-control firmware is a
 separate deliverable from this hardware plan.


 7. Gate driver and inverter connections
 Choose the SPI DRV8353S, specifically the 40-pin RTA package. The RS variant with an integrated buck has
 a different pinout. The included driver table includes all 40 pins and the exposed ground pad. [D8]

 Connect VM to V12 and VDRAIN to VBUS. The high-side charge pump is supplied through the driver's power
 circuitry; it is not powered by the MCU PWM pins. C303 connects CPL to CPH. C304 connects VCP to VDRAIN
 and floats above the bus. Do not connect C304's lower end to ground. VGLS and DVDD have their own
 ground-referenced capacitors. Do not use the driver's DVDD to power the MCU.

 Each gate has its own 4.7-ohm series resistor and 10k gate-to-source pulldown. Gate/source return geometry
 matters more than straight-line placement. Connect SPA/SPB/SPC to their respective shunt top pads, and
 SNA/SNB/SNC to the bottom pads using Kelvin routing. For low-side gate reference, follow the DRV8353
 source-sense arrangement exactly.

 The D2PAK tabs are drains. The high-side tabs are on VBUS; low-side tabs are on three different phases. Any
 common metal heat spreader requires suitable electrical insulation and a verified thermal interface. One
 uninsulated heatsink must not join those nodes.

 Initial driver settings: six-PWM mode; CSA gain 20; bidirectional VREF/2 bias; shunt sensing rather than FET
 sensing; source drive 150 mA and sink drive 300 mA; approximately 2 us drive duration; VDS OCP 0.2 V with
 1 us deglitch and latched fault response. Use a 200 ns driver dead-time setting plus an initial 300 ns MCU
 dead-time request, then measure the resulting effective non-overlap. These are bench starting settings, not
 final optimized values. Read registers back after programming.

 8. Phase-current measurement and ADC timing
 Select three 1-milliohm shunts. With CSA gain 20, the nominal transfer slope magnitude is 20 mV/A. In the
 driver's documented SP-to-SN sign convention, I_shunt = (VREF/2 - VSO)/(20 × 0.001). Confirm the sign
 against actual phase current and PWM state during commissioning; define one consistent motor-current sign
 convention in firmware. [D8]

 At zero current, VSO is approximately 1.65 V. At ±31.11 A it is approximately 1.028 V or 2.272 V. Using a
 0.25–3.05 V usable analog interval gives roughly ±70 A measurement range. A 12-bit ADC with 3.3 V
 reference resolves approximately 0.040 A per count before noise, offset and calibration errors.

 The 1-milliohm resistor dissipates 0.169 W at 13 Arms, or 0.484 W at 22 Arms if it carried a full sinusoid. At
 standstill a phase carrying 31.11 A continuously would dissipate 0.968 W in its shunt when that path
 conducts. PWM duty and cooling determine the real value. The 3 W component rating must be derated at
 operating temperature.

 Use the 47-ohm/1-nF filters at the ADC pins. Perform the driver's offset calibration with PWM blocked,
 followed by ADC zero calibration. Input-referred CSA offset can represent several amperes with a 1-milliohm
 shunt, so raw measurements and hardware trip thresholds need an error budget and bench checks. Validate
 gain using a calibrated current source or current probe.

 A low-side shunt does not continuously observe phase current. Sample only during valid low-side
 conduction windows after switching transients settle. Use simultaneous ADC1/ADC2 conversions for two valid
 phases and reconstruct the third from iU+iV+iW=0. PA0 and PA1 can use either ADC; PA2 uses ADC1, so pair
 W with U or V on ADC2. Do not assume three sequential samples are simultaneous.

 Start at 20 kHz center-aligned PWM, with a conservative modulation ceiling and a target quiet acquisition
 window of at least 3 us. The provisional 90% duty ceiling is not proof that every sector has a valid window.
 Implement sector-dependent sampling and verify it with the actual timer pattern and measured CSA settling.
 If a valid pair is unavailable, reduce modulation or change sampling strategy.

 The study does not supply the incremental inductances needed to calculate switching ripple or PI gains.
 Measure Ld/Lq over useful currents, with the rotor controlled, before enabling 22 Arms pulses. Use the
 measured values for the current-loop model and anti-windup tuning.


 9. Hardware fault chain
 U7/U8 implement six current-window comparisons and bus over/undervoltage. Their open-collector outputs,
 U2 nFAULT, buck power-good outputs and U13 WDO share FAULT_RAW_N. U14/U15 add buffered reset and
 permit signals through Schottky OR diodes. A low on this net clears U9 asynchronously and also asserts
 TIM1_BKIN. Do not tie push-pull outputs directly onto this wired fault net.

  voltage, each phase

 The current window corresponds to about ±39.9 A in the nominal shunt-amplifier convention. It is an
 independent trip target, not a precision current limiter. Include shunt tolerance, CSA gain/offset, resistor
 errors and switching artifacts in its minimum/maximum trip analysis. The driver's VDS protection provides
 additional coverage when low-side shunts are not observing current. Neither the window nor the fuse
 guarantees survival of every short circuit without response-time and MOSFET SOA validation.

 U9 has D and /PRE held high. A deliberate MCU pulse on CLK sets its Q high only after FAULT_RAW_N has
 recovered. The NAND output enables PWM only when Q, MCU_RUN and PERMIT_OK are all high. A trip latches
 motor PWM off; recovery must never automatically resume torque. Keep DRV_ENABLE independent so
 diagnostic registers and current calibration remain accessible with PWM blocked.

 U12 supervises V3V3 with a 3.07 V threshold and an open CT pin for its fixed reset delay. U13's fixed
 watchdog is nominally 200 ms, with the specified tolerance range. This watchdog detects a stalled controller;
 it is too slow to serve as short-circuit protection. The hardware current comparators and driver perform the
 fast path. A software command timeout may be much shorter, for example 20 ms, after system timing is
 established.

 The external permit is a local 3.3 V interface, with a pull-down, RC filter and Schmitt input. An open cable
 disables permission. It can be driven by a local supervisor or a dry contact to the supplied 3.3 V reference.
 This circuit is a functional disable, without a claim of certified Safe Torque Off or redundant safety integrity.


 10. Encoder electrical interface and harness
 The chosen FLUX option uses BIS10 BiSS-C, carried as differential clock and differential data. It does not
 connect directly to the MCU's 3.3 V SPI pins. U18 converts the clock to a differential pair; U20 converts the
 incoming data pair to 3.3 V logic. Both are THVD2450 devices, one configured for transmit and one for
 receive. [D16]

 Use paired conductors for clock and data, plus supply/return, in a shielded harness. Fit the receiving data
 termination on this PCB; 120 ohm is the starting value for a matching cable. Verify the clock receiver's
 built-in termination with FLUX and terminate at the encoder end if necessary. Do not add a second clock
 termination at the transmitting board merely because a footprint exists. The official cable or mating harness
 must be checked for pin numbering and connector orientation before powering it.

 Start the clock at 2 MHz. The BIS10 field comprises 24 position bits, two status bits and a six-bit CRC, with
 separate framing/ACK/start behavior. The 19-bit position is left aligned in the 24-bit field. Its CRC polynomial
 is x^6+x+1, initialized to zero and inverted for transmission. Data changes after rising clock edges and is
 sampled on falling edges. [E3]

 A 32-bit payload occupies 16 us at 2 MHz; include framing, timeout and processing in the 50 us PWM
 schedule. The STM32 SPI/DMA engine can assist data collection, but firmware must implement BiSS framing
 and verify its waveform. Reserve timer/DMA fallback if the required clock and idle timing cannot be
 generated cleanly. Do not treat a plain SPI register read as a completed BiSS implementation.

 Wait for valid encoder startup data before arming; the published power-up allowance is 0.8 s. Keep the
 watchdog serviced during that controlled startup state. Check CRC, warning/error flags, impossible position
 jumps and stale samples on every reading. On loss of valid angle, command zero torque and disable PWM.
 The mechanical angle at the sampling instant must be time-aligned with FOC; compensate deterministic
 delay after measuring it.

 11. Torque sensor, CAN and temperature inputs
 Use a purchased torque sensor with conditioned CAN output for this baseline. J5 and J6 are two connectors
 on the same non-isolated CAN bus, one for the torque sensor and one for the robot controller/daisy chain.
 They do not create two independent CAN channels. The exact sensor protocol, update rate, node ID and
 power voltage are still unselected. Supply that sensor externally until its specification is known.

 The TCAN1042HGV has 5 V bus power and 3.3 V I/O power. Set STB low for normal operation and leave it
 high at reset. Provide 120-ohm termination through a normally open jumper, used only if this board is a
 physical bus endpoint. Keep connector stubs short. If the purchased sensor uses an analog bridge, SPI or
 RS-485, the interface sheet must change; a raw bridge cannot connect to the CAN header. [D9]

 Retain phase-current sensing even with an external torque sensor. FOC needs phase-current information to
 regulate magnetic torque; an output torque sensor measures a different quantity after gearbox losses,
 friction and compliance.

 The board measures one local MOSFET-area NTC and one external winding NTC. The starting contracts are
 10k/B3435 for the board and 10k/B3950 for the winding. Each uses a 10k pull-up and a 1k/100nF ADC filter.
 In firmware use the actual thermistor curve: R_NTC = R_pullup × V/(Vref-V), followed by the selected
 sensor's temperature equation or table. Open and shorted sensors must be faults.

 Initial conservative policies are winding derating above 100 C and stop at 115 C; board derating above 70 C
 and stop at 80 C. These are provisional measured-sensor thresholds. Confirm sensor-to-hotspot error, lag
 and MOSFET junction rise before raising them. The FLUX unit requires its own thermal mounting assessment.


 12. Regenerative brake chopper
 The brake circuit operates from the bus whenever its rails are alive, independently of MCU_RUN. U16
 compares a divided bus signal with REF25. R904 adds positive feedback. U17 switches Q7. Connect the
 external resistor between BRAKE_PLUS and BRAKE_DRAIN. Q7's source returns to the power ground. D2
 returns wire-inductance current to BRAKE_PLUS when Q7 turns off.

 Using ideal 2.5 V reference and 0/3.3 V comparator output:

 V_on = 2.5 × (1 + 300/14 + 300/499) = 57.57 V.

 V_off = V_on - 3.3 × 300/499 = 55.59 V.

 The turn-off level stays above the assumed 54 V maximum supply, providing room for tolerance. Calculate
 worst-case thresholds using the actual reference drift, resistor tolerance, comparator offset and output
 levels. Validate the 470 pF input-filter delay and resulting voltage overshoot before selecting final values.

 The starting 4.7-ohm external resistor draws 12.25 A and dissipates about 705 W while the chopper is on. A
 resistor labelled 200 W cannot absorb 705 W continuously. Its duty cycle, pulse energy, heatsink and thermal
 cutoff must be sized from returned mechanical energy. Even an ideal 200 W continuous allowance limits
 average ON duty to roughly 28%; thermal margins reduce that value.

 Calculate stopping energy from reflected inertia and speed, plus energy delivered by the load: E = 0.5 ×
 J_equivalent × omega_motor^2, adjusted for other moving masses, gravity if relevant, and externally
 imposed motion. The study does not define a complete robot-load inertia or braking duty. Reserve this
 resistor provision in the schematic now and finalize its energy rating with the mechanism model.

 Turning off all six inverter MOSFETs does not isolate a back-driven motor: their body diodes can still return
 energy to VBUS. The brake path and bus disconnect strategy therefore remain necessary even when the
 controller is in a fault state.

 14. PCB layout and thermal constraints
 Use a four-layer starting stack with 2 oz outer copper and continuous inner ground reference. Allocate the
 top layer to compact switching cells and critical components, an inner layer to ground, another to
 power/quiet signals as appropriate, and the bottom to additional copper and control routing. Obtain the
 actual manufacturer stackup before calculating copper temperature rise or impedance.

 Place each half bridge, its shunt and ceramic bus capacitors as a compact cell. Keep the
 VBUS-capacitor/high-FET/low-FET/shunt loop small. Do the same for Q7 and its capacitor. Keep high-dv/dt
 phase copper away from encoders, current-amplifier traces and MCU analog pins. Avoid unnecessarily large
 switch-node copper even when other power polygons need to be broad.

 Route every SP/SN pair from the shunt pads as a true Kelvin pair. The ground-side Kelvin branch is
 electrically GND, but carries only sense current. Do not create a floating analog-ground island or cut the
 ground plane under digital return paths. Keep load current out of the driver and MCU reference connections
 through placement and return-path geometry.

 The first-order bridge conduction estimate, using an assumed hot Rds(on) of 4.8 milliohm, is 3 × Irms^2 ×
 Rds: about 2.43 W at 13 Arms and 6.97 W at 22 Arms. This excludes switching, diode, gate-drive, shunt,
 capacitor and regulator losses. Total average gate charge demand at 20 kHz, using 118 nC per MOSFET, is
 about 14.2 mA. Neither estimate is a thermal proof. [D3]

 Specify copper widths and via arrays from allowable temperature rise, layer thickness, current waveform and
 connection geometry. Do not use a universal width-per-amp rule. Reserve at least 1 mm spacing in the
 power area where practical, while respecting validated component footprints; a final clearance rule also
 depends on the manufacturing and environmental requirements.

 Route critical power, gate-drive, Kelvin and feedback networks manually or with explicit geometry
 constraints. An autorouter's connectivity success does not validate power integrity, heat flow or current
 sampling. Separate the buck switch nodes from the current amplifiers and encoder connector.
